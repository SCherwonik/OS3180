## Model: ln(MPG) ~ ln(weight) + ln(HP) + footprint + hybrid

R^2 = **0.846**, adjusted R^2 = 0.8454, F(4, 1020) = 1401.2 (p = 0.0e+00), n = 1,025 -> 205:1 observations per parameter.

Elasticity reading: a 10% increase in horsepower costs about 4.8% in fuel economy; 10% more weight costs about 3.0%. A hybrid powertrain buys roughly 23% better economy at the same weight, power, and size. VIFs are moderate (max 3.4 on ln_weight): size travels with weight, so footprint's own coefficient stays small once weight is in the model, but no estimate is destabilized -- the multicollinearity slide gets a clean bill with a caveat rather than a problem.

## Limitations

- Cross-sectional, MY2024 only; elasticities describe the 2024 fleet, not a causal redesign experiment.
- Weight/footprint collinearity (VIF above) limits attribution between mass and size.
- 2-cycle lab MPG, not real-world (deliberate: physics DV, single consistent test).
- EVs excluded by measurement unit; the model explains combustion physics, not the full market.
