# Design-Normalized Fuel-Economy Productivity

Generated by 29_productivity.py.

## Construction

MPG_norm(t) = fleet real-world MPG x (weight_t/weight_2024)^0.4527 x (HP_t/HP_2024)^0.3689, elasticities from the stage-33 final equation (pooled, all powertrains). Interpretation: what each year's technology would have delivered at 2024's fleet weight and power.

Worked example, 1987: raw 22.0 MPG at 3221 lbs / 118 HP vs 2024's 4354 lbs / 258 HP -> 22.0 x (3221/4354)^0.4527 x (118/258)^0.3689 = 22.0 x 0.872 x 0.748 = 14.3.

## Findings

- The 1987-2004 MPG trough disappears: normalized productivity is monotone (10.0 -> 14.3 -> 17.4 -> 27.2 for 1975/1987/2004/2024). Raw MPG fell in the middle years only because the gains were spent on weight and power.
- Growth is close to exponential and startlingly steady: **1.79%/yr over 1975-2024 (R2 = 0.980)**; 2.19%/yr over 2005-2024 (R2 = 0.985).

## Caveats (present with the chart)

- Elasticities are estimated on the 2024 cross-section and applied to five decades: a stability assumption, partially supported by the era refits (R2 0.71-0.78 across 2000+/2020+ windows).
- Fleet real-world MPG folds EV energy-equivalents in, so part of post-2011 'productivity' is electrification mix, not combustion technology; the projection inherits that blend and effectively assumes the electrification pace continues.
- Straight-line extrapolation in log space: no physics ceiling, no policy scenario. The two rates bracket, not bound, the future.
- Slope-CI band on the chart reflects fit uncertainty only, not scenario uncertainty, which dominates.
